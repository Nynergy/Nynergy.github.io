For a long time, I've enjoyed music that lasts a while. Music that stays, or is in no hurry to leave, but never overstays its welcome. My love of ambient and drone music certainly shows in this regard, but there are plenty of records outside those genres that I also love despite their lengthy runtimes.

I like music I can sit with. Music I can melt into. Music that sticks to me after it is gone. Music that demands something of me. Music that commands my attention. Music that lets my mind wander.

I want to talk about albums I love that are over an hour long. Maybe you'll like them, too. For the sake of brevity (ironic, given the subject matter) I'll limit myself to 10 records.

Post-Rock Takes Its Time (I)

Mogwai - Young Team (1997) 1 Hour, 4 Minutes
'Young Team' was one of the first post-rock albums I heard during my middle school years that made me go "What the heck was that?" It wouldn't be until high school that I actually fell in love with the dynamic, eccentric, soft-spoken classic.

Slow-burning meditations of guitar, bass, and drums mix with recordings of people speaking, taking up minutes of beautifully meandering time. But don't be fooled by the softness of a track like "Tracy", other songs like "Summer" and "Like Herod" will bash you over the head with sudden intensity. Other songs like "With Portfolio" still leave me scratching my head, despite the smile on my face.

Of course, the album isn't complete without the iconic 16-minute closing track "Mogwai Fear Satan" and its roiling guitar, flute, and percussion. A record that reminds me "music is bigger than words, and wider than pictures."

It's Called 'Slowcore' For a Reason

Bluetile Lounge - Half Cut (1998) 1 Hour, 5 Minutes
This album is an emotional powerhouse with a glacial pace. At first, it may sound like the band is just kind of hitting a drum or strumming a guitar whenever they feel like it in order to break up the quiet tension, but as you continue listening you'll pick out the structures of each song, stretched and extended to the point of shattering.

While the music can be heavy and intense, it's almost heavier in the smaller, quieter moments, such as the ending of a track like "Lapsis". Sounds for a rainy autumn day, for a sad season, for staying in bed and staring at nothing in particular.

Heavy or Not

Boris - Flood (2000) 1 Hour, 10 Minutes
Boris are no strangers to heavy music. Their discography is filled with countless classic records that exude so much heaviness they could put a crater in the earth. 'Flood' has its heavy moments, notably "Flood III", but it's the extended, repetitive moments of the album that really capture my attention, and let it wander all the same.

"Flood I" is a study in phasing, as a simple guitar lick repeats at two slightly different speeds for many minutes. Drums come washing in and out, eventually leading to the beautifully bare guitar and drums of "Flood II". After the epic heaviness of "Flood III" has passed, "Flood IV" takes us out into the deep ocean, slowly fading away until you forget you were even listening to music in the first place.

Even the Title is Long

The Olivia Tremor Control - Music From The Unrealized Film Script: Dusk At Cubist Castle (1996) 1 Hour, 14 Minutes
This classic release from the Elephant 6 Collective is a mix of psychedelic pop, indie rock, and field recordings. This is a weird one, but a rewarding one nonetheless. Most of the album is filled with wonderfully strange and jangly rock tunes that are fun and catchy, but there are also some longer meditative passages that ask the listener to sit within the sound and let it take them where it wishes.

The "Green Typewriters" suite is a medley of different psychedelic passages, including an ambient section, that feels like the stages of an acid trip. The title track (if you can call it that, given the lack of "Film Script" prefix) is another longer ambient track. This album is a trip, in more ways than one, and a worthwhile one at that.

Long Drive, Life Passing By

Big Thief - Dragon New Warm Mountain I Believe In You (2022) 1 Hour, 20 Minutes
This album feels like a life lived in the span of a year. So many different emotions and stories told in this collection of folk rock tunes. It feels like the soundtrack to a long drive where you have nothing to think about but the state of your life and all the people in it.

This album makes me believe in love, joy, and the future. Long runtime be damned, I don't want the record to end.

Epic Journeys Abound

Lac Observation - A City of Gandharvas (2025) 1 Hour, 26 Minutes
This record is heavily influenced by The Olivia Tremor Control, which I discussed earlier. It's slightly less psychedelic, but tends to lean more heavily into extended song structures and fantasy moods.

It's a concept record about going on an epic journey to a city in the clouds, and all the trials and tribulations you face along the way. The songs are great, and the vibes are fantastical. Not only does the album contain a 20-minute ambient "interlude" about traveling through a white mist that makes you forget things, that passage ends with a short section called "Elf Parade" that sounds just as strange and whimsical as the title suggests.

Post-Rock Takes Its Time (II)

Godspeed You! Black Emperor - Lift Your Skinny Fists Like Antennas to Heaven (2000) 1 Hour, 27 Minutes
Another entry in the "long album name" category, 'Lift Your Skinny Fists' is a titan of the post-rock world. Handily my favorite record of the genre, its four tracks all hover relatively close to the 20-minute mark, and they make ample use of that time.

The album is cinematic, stuffed with incredible instrumental passages, field recordings, ambient movements, and more. A riveting record that feels more like watching a movie than listening to an album, and keeps me hooked all the same. There is something about the closing passage of "Sleep" that will always be a life-affirming moment for me.

Glacial Drones

Kali Malone - The Sacrificial Code (2019) 1 Hour, 46 Minutes
Kali Malone altered my brain chemistry with this release of long-form organ drones, each one moving at a glacial pace. Progressions change slowly and cyclically, lulling you into a trance. The way that these organs were recorded brings out the physical nature and timbres of the pipes in a way that I find ultimately pleasing.

I could melt into this record forever. It's minimal and austere, but with attentive listening you can peel back the layers of white to find so many colors beneath. "Spectacle of Ritual" is one of the most beautiful compositions I've heard, and is a perfect opener to the album.

A Lengthy Tapestry Weaved

Joanna Newsom - Have One On Me (2010) 2 Hours, 4 Minutes
Joanna's follow-up to 2006's 'Ys' is a triple-LP filled with her signature vocals and harp playing, with a nice dash of Americana thrown into some songs as well. We've passed the 2-hour mark here, but this album earns every second of that runtime, with songs that come from a raw and poetic place of vulnerability and self-awareness.

I still find myself humming the melodies of many songs from this record, which is a testament to Joanna's writing chops, that even in a tracklist this long, there are standout moments that stick with you long after.

Summer Days Stretched

the volume settings folder - Ghosts In The Heat (2022) 2 Hours, 19 Minutes
Rounding out our list is a favorite of mine in the ambient space. "Ghosts In The Heat" is a 2-hour selection of summer vignettes, soft moods, tape loops, and atmospheres that bring me to a comfortable place in my mind.

You don't have to listen to this record front-to-back, shuffling the tracks gives you much the same experience. It's a record to sit inside of as you exist in the real world. It reminds me of hot summer days, twilight clouds, the murmur of leaves, times of my childhood.

If you want something like this record that feels a bit cooler, a bit sparser, but still scratches the same itch, I would recommend the "sister" album to this one, which tvsf just released a few days ago as of writing this, called 'Samizdat', which clocks in at a similar length.

There are plenty of other records I want to talk about in this category, such as Unwound's 'Leaves Turn Inside You' or Mount Eerie's 'Night Palace', but for now I'll leave it at these 10. Maybe in the future I'll do more of these kinds of blog posts.

Until then, give these records a listen. Hopefully you find something you like.
